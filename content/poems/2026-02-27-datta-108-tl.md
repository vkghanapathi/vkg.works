abstract: Śrī Datta Śatakam (The Hundred Verses on Dattātreya) is a devotional Sanskrit
  composition by Vamshi Krishna Ghanapāṭhī dedicated to Lord Dattātreya, composed
  during the Vikṛti samvat year on the occasion of Devī Śaradnavaratri and Dattātreya
  Jayantī (13 October 2010). The work comprises one hundred verses of praise structured
  thematically around the avatāra (descent), birth narrative, epithets, supremacy,
  the Vedic association, trinitarian attributes, six marks of liberation, the yoga
  of non-attachment, and the advaitic principle of the divine guru Dattātreya as Brahman
  (Saccidānanda).
preamble: This work belongs to the Advaita Vedānta devotional tradition centered on
  Dattātreya, the guru-deity recognized in Hindu philosophy as the incarnation of
  the Trimūrti (Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva united). Dattātreya occupies a central place
  in tantric, yogic, and renunciate (avadhūta) traditions, and is venerated as the
  supreme teacher of non-dual wisdom. Composed in the classical Sanskrit meter-verse
  (ślokabandhana) tradition, this śataka follows the established practice of one-hundred-verse
  homologies dedicated to deities and gurus. The work invokes the blessings of Ganapatī
  Saccidānanda Swami and frames itself as an offering during a major festival calendar
  event.

- mula: 'दत्तात्रेय स्वभाव प्रकटन पटिम प्रोज्ज्वलद् ज्ञानभासः

    सर्वात्मत्वा न्मदीयां जडधिय मपि योऽनाविलां चर्करीति।

    उत्सोत्पन्नाभ वाचं समुदयतु गुरु र्मामकीनो महेज्यः

    तद्ब्रह्मण्यं भजेऽहं गणपति यमिनं सच्चिदानन्द दत्तम्।।'
  padavibhaga: दत्तात्रेय | स्वभाव | प्रकटन | पटिम | प्रोज्ज्वलत् | ज्ञानभासः | सर्वात्मत्वात्
    | मदीयां | जडधियम् | अपि | यः | अनाविलाम् | चर्करीति | उत्सोत्पन्नाभः | वाचं |
    समुदयतु | गुरुः | मामकीनः | महेज्यः | तत्ब्रह्मण्यम् | भजे | अहं | गणपति | यमिनम्
    | सच्चिदानन्द | दत्तम्।।
  anvaya: यः दत्तात्रेय स्वभाव प्रकटन पटिम प्रोज्ज्वलत् ज्ञानभासः सर्वात्मत्वात् मदीयां
    जडधियम् अपि अनाविलाम् चर्करीति, उत्सोत्पन्नाभः मामकीनः महेज्यः गुरुः वाचं समुदयतु,
    अहं तत्ब्रह्मण्यं गणपति यमिनं सच्चिदानन्द दत्तं भजे।
  pratipadartha: दत्तात्रेय = dattātreya (Dattatreya); स्वभाव = svabhāva (nature);

    (shining forth); ज्ञानभासः = jñānabhāsaḥ (light of knowledge); सर्वात्मत्वात्
    = sarvātmatvāt (due to being the Self of all); मदीयां = madīyāṃ (my); जडधियम्
    = jaḍadhiyam (dull intellect); अपि = api (even); यः = yaḥ (who); अनाविलाम् = anāvilām
    (pure); चर्करीति = carkarīti (makes); उत्सोत्पन्नाभः = utsotpannābhaḥ (like born
    from a spring); वाचं = vācaṃ (speech); समुदयतु = samudayatu (may elevate); गुरुः
    = guruḥ (the guru); मामकीनः = māmakīnaḥ (mine); महेज्यः = mahejyaḥ (greatly worshipful);
    तत्ब्रह्मण्यम् = tadbrahmaṇyaṃ (devoted to Brahman); भजे = bhaje (I worship);
    अहं = ahaṃ (I); गणपति = gaṇapati (Ganapati); यमिनम् = yaminam (ascetic); सच्चिदानन्द
    = saccidānanda (Satchidananda); दत्तम् = dattam (Datta).
  rupa_nishpatti: चर्करीति — चर्कृ √कृ (to do), reduplicated form, present tense,
    3rd person, singular; सर्वात्मत्वात् — sarvātmatva + tṛtīyā vibhakti, ekavacanam;

  bhavartha_en: I worship that Satchidananda Datta, who is Ganapati, the ascetic,
    and who, by being the Self of all, purifies even my dull intellect with the shining
    light of knowledge that manifests the nature of Dattatreya's skill. May that greatly
    worshipful Guru, like a spring of speech, elevate my words. I worship Him, devoted
    to Brahman.
  commentary: This verse is a prayer to Dattatreya, specifically in the form of Satchidananda
    Datta, who is also identified with Ganapati. The verse emphasizes the power of
    the Guru to purify the intellect and elevate speech. The phrase "Dattātreyasvabhāva-prakaṭana-paṭima-projjvalat-jñānabhāsaḥ"
    highlights the Guru's ability to reveal the true nature of Dattatreya, which is
    the ultimate knowledge. The comparison of the Guru to a spring of speech suggests
    the spontaneous and ever-flowing nature of divine wisdom.
