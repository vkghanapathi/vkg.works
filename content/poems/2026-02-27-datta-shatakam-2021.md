abstract: Śrī Datta Śatakam is a devotional hymnic composition of one hundred stanzas
  (śataka) celebrating the incarnate form and salvific grace of Dattātreya, the Vedic
  teacher-deity of Advaita Vedānta tradition. Each verse employs the classical refrain
  'tat brahmaṇyaṁ bhajēhaṁ mama hṛdi sadayaṁ saccidānanda dattam' and systematically
  unfolds theological themes including Dattātreya's divine descent, namesake glories,
  association with Vedic knowledge, metaphysical attributes, and role as universal
  guru across multiple cosmic ages.
preamble: This work belongs to the sampradāya (doctrinal lineage) of Advaita Vedānta,
  specifically the Dattātreya tradition which venerates the trinitarian embodiment
  of Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and Maheśvara as the supreme nondual Self (Brahman). Dattātreya
  is revered as a paramaguru (supreme preceptor) and incarnate guide to liberation,
  whose historical manifestation (traditionally dated to the Kharṇ era) is commemorated
  annually. The stotram format (invocatory verse) employs Sanskrit's sophisticated
  metrical and philosophical vocabulary to render metaphysical doctrine accessible
  through devotional meditation, a characteristic pedagogy of Vedāntic compositions
  addressed to sincere disciples.

translation_en: 'With the divine blessings of the most revered

  Sri Ganapati Sachchidananda Swamiji,

  the presiding pontiff of Avadhuta Datta Peetham,

  this Sri Datta Shatakam is composed and offered at His holy presence.

  Re-printed on the auspicious occasion of Dattatreya Jayanti in the year Khara.


  A Humble Submission:

  May that great preceptor, Sri Ganapati Yamini Sachchidananda Datta, whose effulgent
  wisdom illuminates the very nature of Dattatreya, and who, being the Self of all,
  stirs even my dull intellect into unwavering contemplation, inspire the wellspring
  of my speech. I adore that Brahman-abiding sage.

  May Vamshikrishna, desiring to attain the wisdom born of the grace of the Guru,
  offer this garland of a hundred gem-like verses, acquired through long service to
  the Guru, at the lotus feet of Sri Guru, thereby pleasing Him.


  Avatāroddeshaḥ (Purpose of the Incarnation)


  1.  I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who incarnated on earth, the unparalleled Lord, for the upliftment of all
  people, and who manifested Himself to proclaim the glory of the Guru''s seat.


  2.  I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who was revealed to Anasuya and Atri, ascetics of immeasurable glory on the
  Riksha mountain, to bestow grace upon the world, and who was born as Atri''s son
  on the full moon day of Margashirsha, for the establishment of auspiciousness in
  the world.


  3.  I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who accepted the name ''Dattatreya,'' supremely established and capable in
  benefiting the world, skilled in protecting devotees at all times, all-powerful,
  and most venerable, a name that is pleasing, delightful, and purifying, praised
  by knowers of Brahman.


  4.  I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, the mere remembrance of whose most excellent name reduces heaps of sins to
  ashes, whose most beloved prayer generates unparalleled merit, and whose most profound
  awakening, when contemplated, ferries one across the great ocean of worldly existence.


  5.  I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who is greater than the creators themselves, generating the sentiments of
  wisdom and detachment in the minds of those who seek refuge, who is greater than
  the controllers, bestowing countless riches upon those who bow down, and who is
  greater than the givers, being most potent in destroying the desires of the mind.


  6.  I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who, as the embodiment of eternal, unchanging consciousness, the substratum
  of the universe, the non-dual reality, delights in the company of dogs, proclaiming
  the principle that the one, true, supreme Brahman, the Self of all, manifests in
  countless beings.


  7.  I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who, as the embodiment of the three forms, reveals the nature of Hari with
  the lotus and the chakra in His hands, who holds the trident and the damaru in His
  middle hands, signifying the nature of Shambhu, and who bears the marks of Brahma,
  holding the rosary, the water pot, and the sacred thread, ever engaged in the repetition
  of the mantra.


  8.  I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who, by His mere glance, dispels the six modifications of being – the changes
  of existence – that are perceived in this world due to illusion, which, being effects
  of Maya, cause the delusion of reality through experience, and who Himself is immutable
  and free from change.


  9.  I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who, if He chooses to give, the two hands are not sufficient to receive;
  who bestows liberation from bondage in the case of the desireless, and fulfills
  all desires in the case of those with desires. What more can I say? He, abounding
  in compassion, grants the supreme bliss of Sachchidananda.


  10. I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who initiated Himself into the state of Avadhuta, renouncing the attachments
  to honor, one''s own place, wife, son, cattle, house, heaven, fame, and other desires,
  for the sake of the wisdom and detachment of countless devotees, and who is the
  light of the essential truth of the creeper of the Vedic scriptures.


  11. I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who, by superimposing the signs of wisdom, such as humility, on twenty-four
  objects in this world, revealed the greatness of being the Jagadguru, the preceptor
  of the world, illuminating the path of true knowledge.


  12. I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who, when all people were being scorched and tormented in the forest fire
  of worldly existence, was approached by King Yadu, who, like an elephant immersed
  in the waters of the Ganges, sought inner peace, and who, being an ocean of wisdom,
  was earnestly requested to impart the secret of limitless joy.


  13. I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who, by constantly teaching through the various qualities and actions of
  the objects of the three worlds, which are subject to acceptance and rejection,
  instructed that one should carefully examine everything with subtle discernment
  and experience supreme bliss, realizing, "I am not the ignorant one."


  14. I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who declared that the earth is the first and foremost guru, enduring all
  that is done to her, remaining steadfast, bearing everything with patience, and
  giving her all for the sake of others, teaching the principle of selfless service
  and endurance.


  15. I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who declared that the wind is my guru, benefiting the entire world by its
  association with all objects, carrying both the fragrance and the foulness, yet
  remaining detached, thus teaching the principle of non-attachment.


  16. I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who, being all-pervading like the sky, appears to be different due to pots,
  monasteries, and bellies, but is in reality vast, limitless, and without beginning
  or end, and who, as the embodiment of the Self, declared, "Thus is the infinitude
  of the Self realized."


  17. I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who declared that the waters are my gurus, shining brightly, compassionate,
  pure, pleasing, colorless, gentle, devoid of impurities, and said to be the essence
  of taste, thus teaching that one should attain purity like them and become a sage.


  18. I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who declared that the fire, with its seven flames, consuming everything,
  accepts even the position of my guru, burning away all impurities and destroying
  all that is evil by its radiance, thus teaching that one should attain the purity
  of fire.


  19. I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who declared that the moon, adorned with spots, accepts the position of my
  guru, showing the increase and decrease of its phases, and that the body carries
  birth and death, ever proceeding towards death, thus teaching that the visible is
  perishable, but the Self is eternal.


  20. I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who declared that the sun is the teacher, appearing differently in the daytime
  with its various rays, appearing different in pots, rivers, lakes, and waterfalls,
  yet remaining one and the same, and that the Self resides in various beings, thus
  teaching that one should recognize the Self in all.


  21. I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who declared that the bird is the instructor, its sweet sound silenced by
  death at the hands of the hunter, and that the female bird, caught in the net, is
  seen suffering with her young ones, thus teaching that delusion leads to sorrow,
  and that one should awaken to the desire for liberation.


  22. I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who declared that the ocean, in whose deep depths lie countless jewels and
  immeasurable treasures, remains calm and unperturbed, whether the streams flowing
  into it are small or large, thus teaching that one should attain the depth and tranquility
  of the ocean.


  23. I adore that Brahman-abiding Sachchidananda Datta, ever compassionate in my
  heart, who declared that the python, content with whatever comes its way, whether
  little or much, delicious or not, remains happy and at ease, thus teaching that
  one should be content with whatever food is obtained.'
