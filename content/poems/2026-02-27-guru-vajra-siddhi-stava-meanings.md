abstract: Guru Siddhi Stotram is a devotional hymn celebrating the eight supernatural
  perfections (ashta-siddhis) embodied in the spiritual preceptor. Organized as a
  series of prayers addressing each siddhi—starting with Garima (weightiness/majesty),
  Mahima (greatness), and Anima (minuteness)—the work combines Vedantic philosophy
  with devotional rhetoric, interpreting the guru's transformative power through the
  lens of classical scriptural commentary (vyākhyā) traditions and employing detailed
  etymological analysis of Sanskrit terms.
preamble: This work belongs to the Hindu guru-yoga tradition, wherein the preceptor
  is venerated as the embodiment of divine knowledge and liberating grace. Rooted
  in the Upanishadic understanding of the guru-disciple relationship and elaborated
  through later Advaita and bhakti schools, the Guru Siddhi Stotram applies the metaphysical
  framework of the eight yogic perfections—traditionally associated with divine or
  ascetic attainment—to the spiritual authority of the sadguru (true teacher). The
  composition uses systematic Sanskrit exegesis to unfold both the philosophical meaning
  and the transformative function of each siddhi, presenting the guru as the gateway
  to sat-chit-ananda (being-consciousness-bliss).

translation_en: 'Guru Siddhi Stotram


  Introduction: The Guru is endowed with eight siddhis. To proclaim that He is the
  target of faultless words adorned with all essences, the greatness of the Sadguru,
  known as Garima Siddhi, is first explained in the manner of Guru-hood.


  1.  He who, as the sovereign of Garima, descended to Earth for the sake of mankind,
  filling people with abundant and delightful bliss, diminishing the great sorrow
  of worldly existence, shattering the stone of ignorance, may He, whose brilliance
  is like a thunderbolt, lead us to the state of Sat-Chit-Ananda. Thus, He is praised
  by us. May He grant us the essence of Sat-Chit-Ananda, so that we may conclude our
  lives in Sat-Chit-Ananda. Or, may He become Sat-Chit-Ananda, transforming those
  devoid of that experience into beings full of Sat-Chit-Ananda.


  It is described that the Guru''s power is like a thunderbolt, by which even the
  hard stone-like ignorance is shattered, thus removing the disease of worldly existence,
  bestowing indescribable great bliss, and enabling the dwelling in the earthly body
  through the experience of the Sat-Chit-Ananda Parabrahman. May this sequence of
  liberation be obtained from the Sadguru.


  Introduction: Now, the manner of the Sadguru''s Siddhi known as Mahima is revealed
  in the form of leading to the path of the meaning of the Vedas.


  2.  He who, as the sovereign of Mahima, is Himself the uplifter of all leaders,
  guiding the worlds exceedingly to the essence of the infinite Vedas, becoming the
  basis of sacrifices, directing the virtuous to the celestial realm, may He, whose
  brilliance is like a thunderbolt, lead us to the bliss of Sat-Chit-Ananda. He elevates
  all leaders. How does He guide? By making them attain the essence of the infinite
  Vedas. The meaning of the Vedas culminates in action and Brahman. Therefore, by
  propagating the meaning of the Vedas, He makes the ignorant fully adorned with Vedic
  principles, thereby uplifting all people.


  How is this achieved? It is clarified that the Guru is indeed Govinda. Becoming
  the basis of sacrifices (Yajno vai Vishnu). He is the Supreme Self, the basis of
  all virtuous deeds. After performing sacrifices, He directs the virtuous, the performers
  of sacrifices, to the celestial realm. Such a Guru Govinda, whose brilliance is
  like a thunderbolt, being praised by us, may He lead us to Sat-Chit-Ananda. May
  He cause us to attain Sat-Chit-Ananda. Or, abiding as the indwelling Self, may He
  Himself attain it.


  Introduction: Hereafter, the Sadguru''s Siddhi known as Anima is described as eradicating
  the tendency towards sin.


  3.  He who attains this Anima, the subtle essence, for the sake of uprooting the
  very root of sins, heals and reforms the groups of minds shattered by the fatigue
  of ancient, sinful fever, being the divine physician who bestows medicine, may He,
  whose brilliance is like a thunderbolt, be the singer of Sat-Chit-Ananda. He attains
  this subtlety for the sake of uprooting the very root of sins. He heals. Whom? Those
  whose minds are shattered by the fatigue of ancient, sinful fever. He heals those
  groups of minds. And what does He do again? He reforms. By eradicating the tendencies
  of the mind that cause sin, He reforms. How is He? The divine physician who bestows
  medicine. He is directly the Supreme Lord. The Guru, the embodiment of that, being
  praised by us, may He be the singer and praiser of Sat-Chit-Ananda for us.


  Introduction:

  Now, the Sadguru''s Siddhi known as Laghima is described as the impeller of those
  skilled in good deeds.


  4.  He who, through the path of Laghima, grants cessation from the unbroken cycle
  of worldly existence, being the great one whose order of beneficence is the impelling
  and assisting of the assembly of those who desire to accomplish the most important
  tasks, may He, whose brilliance is like a thunderbolt, be the worshipper of Sat-Chit-Ananda.
  He grants cessation from the unbroken, immense cycle of worldly existence. He causes
  cessation from the unbroken flow of worldly existence. By what path? Through the
  path of Laghima, the Siddhi. And again, He impels and assists the assembly of those
  who desire to accomplish the most important tasks. He impels and supports the virtuous
  who are engaged in performing deeds beneficial to the world. The Guru, the Supreme
  Lord, being praised by us, may He be the worshipper, promoter, and encourager of
  Sat-Chit-Ananda for us.


  Introduction: Now, the Sadguru''s Siddhi known as Prapti, and the subjugation through
  the practice of Yoga, are praised.


  5.  He who is the sovereign of intense Yoga, and therefore possesses the attainment
  of incomparable objects, being intent on dispelling the poverty of those who have
  taken refuge in Him, relying on the sharpest intellect, and fulfilling the intended
  tasks, may He, whose brilliance is like a thunderbolt, be the protector of Sat-Chit-Ananda.
  He is the king of intense Yoga, the Yogeshwara. Therefore, He possesses the attainment
  of incomparable objects. From that wealth, He is intent on dispelling the poverty
  of His devotees. He increases the wealth of the devotees. In what manner? Relying
  on the sharpest intellect. He fulfills the intended tasks through His own planning
  and intellectual skill. Or, for the sake of those who rely on the sharpest intellect,
  He accomplishes His own tasks. Thus, the possession of the Siddhi of Prapti by the
  Sadguru shines forth. The Guru, the Lord, being praised by us, may He be the protector
  of Sat-Chit-Ananda.'
