abstract: A devotional stotra (hymn of praise) to Hanuman composed by Dr. Vamshi Krishna
  Ghanapāṭhī, structured as eight verses of salutation followed by a phalaśruti (statement
  of benefits) and a closing invocation. The work emphasizes Hanuman's protection
  of the devotee through the metaphor of the single-eyed glance (ekanetre-kaṭākṣeṇa),
  presenting him as cosmic protector, dharma-guardian, and compassionate ally of the
  afflicted.

  of Rama and embodiment of courage, loyalty, and dharmic duty. Composed in both Sanskrit
  and Telugu, the work engages classical Vedic hymnic conventions—particularly the
  repetition of a refrain and the concluding phalaśruti—while addressing contemporary
  spiritual needs including protection from supernatural afflictions, economic hardship,
  and existential fear. The author, a scholar-practitioner of Vedic recitation (ghanapāṭha),
  situates the hymn within the living tradition of devotional worship and mantra practice.

translation_en: 'Netikanti Anjaneya Swami Stotram


  1.  I seek refuge in Hanuman, who pervades the universe, dwelling in mountains and
  forests, whose single-eyed glance protects the world.


  2.  I seek refuge in that monkey, who, in the form of Anandatirtha, protects devotees
  and mountain folk with a single-eyed glance.


  3.  I take refuge in Maruti, who stands with a single-eyed glance, intent on protecting
  those engaged in their duties, with a vow to safeguard Dharma.


  4.  I seek refuge in the beautiful one, who with a single-eyed glance, protects
  the poor, the rich, the afflicted, the deprived, and the unauthorized.


  5.  I adore Hari, who with a single-eyed glance, destroys the suffering, disease,
  fear, and the like, caused by ghosts, planets, and demons.


  6.  I worship the boon-giver, who is pleased by recitations of Pavamana mantras
  and the chanting of the Manyu Sukta, with a single-eyed glance.


  7.  I follow as Guru, him who directs with a single-eyed glance, the invincible
  Sri Ramadasa, a scholar of the nine Vyākaraṇas.


  8.  I seek refuge in Anjaneya, the establisher of Dharma, the divine one, of great
  splendor, who with a single-eyed glance, protects the country.


  Phalaśruti (The Fruit of Listening):


  Surely, a good day dawns for one who recites this stotram, by the grace of Hanuman,
  who is pleased and of unparalleled benevolence.


  Prayer:


  I seek refuge in Maruti, the servant at the feet of the best of the Raghu dynasty,
  solely dedicated to the protection of Dharma, the cause of joy for the revered daughter
  of Janaka, the great one, far from the fear of death, the friend of devotees, compassionate,
  and the giver of the supreme state.


  Plava Samvat, Magha Shukla Dashami

  Vamshikrishna Ghanapathi

  11-02-2022

  Mysore'
