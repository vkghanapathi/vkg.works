abstract: A devotional stotra (hymn of praise) to Hanuman composed by Dr. Vamshi Krishna
  Ghanapāṭhī, structured as eight verses of salutation followed by a phalaśruti (statement
  of benefits) and a closing invocation. The work emphasizes Hanuman's protection
  of the devotee through the metaphor of the single-eyed glance (ekanetre-kaṭākṣeṇa),
  presenting him as cosmic protector, dharma-guardian, and compassionate ally of the
  afflicted.

  on Hanuman, the monkey deity revered throughout Hindu practice as the supreme devotee
  of Rama and embodiment of courage, loyalty, and dharmic duty. Composed in both Sanskrit
  and Telugu, the work engages classical Vedic hymnic conventions—particularly the
  repetition of a refrain and the concluding phalaśruti—while addressing contemporary
  spiritual needs including protection from supernatural afflictions, economic hardship,
  and existential fear. The author, a scholar-practitioner of Vedic recitation (ghanapāṭha),
  situates the hymn within the living tradition of devotional worship and mantra practice.

translation_en: 'Netikanti Anjaneya Swami Stotram


  1.  I seek refuge in Hanuman, who pervades the universe, dwelling in mountains and
  forests, whose single-eyed glance protects the world.


  2.  I seek refuge in that monkey, who, in the form of Anandatirtha, protects devotees
  and mountain folk with a single-eyed glance.


  3.  I take refuge in Maruti, who stands ready with a vow to protect Dharma, to rescue
  those engaged in their duties, with a single-eyed glance.


  4.  I seek refuge in the beautiful one, who protects the poor, the rich, the afflicted,
  the deceived, and the powerless with a single-eyed glance.


  5.  I worship Hari, who destroys the suffering caused by ghosts, planets, demons,
  diseases, fears, and the like, with a single-eyed glance.


  6.  I worship the boon-granting one, who is pleased by recitations of Pavamana mantras
  and the chanting of Manyu Sukta, with a single-eyed glance.


  7.  I follow as my Guru, the invincible Sri Ramadasa, a scholar in the nine Vyākaraṇas,
  who bestows grace with a single-eyed glance.


  8.  I seek refuge in Anjaneya, the establisher of Dharma, the divine one, the possessor
  of great splendor, the protector of the country, with a single-eyed glance.


  Phalaśruti (Benefit of Recitation):


  Surely, one who recites this stotram will have an auspicious day, by the grace of
  Hanuman, who is supremely pleased and grants immeasurable blessings.


  Prayer:


  I seek refuge in Maruti, the servant at the feet of Rama, solely dedicated to the
  protection of Dharma, the cause of joy for the revered daughter of Janaka, the great
  one, far from the fear of death, the friend of devotees, compassionate, and the
  giver of the supreme state.


  Written by Vamśīkṛṣṇa Ghanapāṭhī on Magha Shuddha Dashami of Plava Samvat, 11-02-2022,
  in Mysore.'
