abstract: A devotional poem sequence of fourteen verses addressed to Śrī Rāma as Yogeśvara
  (the Lord of Yoga), systematically addressing the stages of yogic practice and the
  eventual dissolution of the physical body. The work guides the practitioner through
  embodied illusion, sense withdrawal, vital breath control, and final liberation,
  culminating in ritual prescriptions for the departed soul's journey and the proper
  disposition of earthly wealth and attachments.
preamble: This composition belongs to the Sanskrit devotional and philosophical poetry
  tradition of Hindu Yoga practice, specifically within the framework of Advaita Vedānta
  and tantric approaches to liberation. The verses are structured as a meditation
  manual addressed to Rāma in his form as Yogeśvara, the supreme teacher of yoga who
  embodies both transcendent consciousness and the knowledge of embodied practice.
  The work draws on Upaniṣadic teachings on the nature of self (ātman), the dissolution
  of identification with the body-mind complex, and the proper discharge of funeral
  and ancestral rituals (śrāddha and pinda karma) as specified in Vedic protocols.

translation_en: 'O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga, may I ever find the wisdom of profound meaning!


  1.  **Dispelling the Illusion of the Body**

  Forgetting utterly the unified state, "I am Brahman," all people with reverence
  embrace the steadfast feeling of belonging to this body. Abandoning this delusion
  born of hunger, thirst, and disease, may I awaken to my true form, to the unfailing
  essence, O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  2.  **The Departure of the Life Force**

  At the moment of life''s departure, the senses weaken, and due to a state of drowsiness,
  one is weary, unable to exert oneself, ceasing from one''s duties, thinking, "I
  am not, I am not." Every sense, indeed, performs its action until the end, by the
  will of its impeller. May I ever find the wisdom of profound meaning, O Śrīrāma,
  Lord of Yoga.


  3.  **The Dissolution of the Senses**

  Merge speech into the mind, and the mind into the life-breaths; then quickly dissolve
  them into the element of fire, and that into the vast, primordial Self, step by
  step. In the arising of the illusion of identifying with the body, worship Hari,
  without wavering or fear. Thus is the desire to know the path of liberation, O Śrīrāma,
  Lord of Yoga.


  4.  **The Life-Breath and Death**

  Who is this, ever present and hidden, renowned by the name of life-breath? What
  will be his name when he abandons the body—death, or time itself? Though dwelling
  within, or even without, he always bestows happiness upon the living. May I contemplate
  that source, that abode, as my own, O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  5.  **Specific Departures and Journeys**

  Though you are established on a long path, O father, whether to earth, sky, or heaven,
  or even to the realm of hope, may you attain whatever your mind desires—nectar,
  or herbs. Whatever form you wish to enter, enter it; but may I be protected by you,
  ever devoted to the path of my own duty, O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  6.  **The Body''s Ritual: The Tripartite Division**

  Lay the body on the earth, perform the rites by fire, and cast the remaining bones
  into the waters of the streams, as the air departs to the sky from the body. This
  frame, made tripartite of earth, water, and fire, again dissolves the effect into
  the cause, here is the word of Vedic ordinance, O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  7.  **What is Beautiful? The Body or its Creation?**

  The body appears pleasing in form, adorned with color and measure, but beauty in
  the effect is improper; the cause is beautiful. It is very difficult to see with
  eyes of flesh, due to its subtle essence. May I behold beauty with the heart, O
  Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  8.  **The Happiness Born of the Senses is False**

  Reflecting on where inertness, happiness, and sorrow reside, by investigating the
  inner root, one finds the semblance of a source of happiness. The root of happiness
  and sorrow is uprooted, understood from the wisdom dwelling within me. Nothing can
  diminish the bliss of my own Self, O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  9.  **The Fear of Solitude**

  The world, with its varied minds, greatly fears the sight of another, for it seeks
  to harm, coming from the fear of otherness in solitude. One constantly fears even
  solitude, without the discrimination of consciousness. But you, indeed, wander in
  the bliss of solitude, in truth, O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  10. **The Cause of the Fear of Solitude**

  What can one different from us do here, with objects that cause sorrow? There is
  no true connection, due to the absence of similarity. See everything as illusory;
  what can destroy what, anywhere? By time alone comes the transformation of name
  and form, O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  11. **The Assemblage and Yama, the Son of Vivasvān**

  He who is without even a trace of drowsiness, righteous, and a performer of good
  deeds, who has approached the endless, according to the proper order of the births
  of all beings. May an oblation be offered first to that Yama in the Ahavaniya fire.
  May the rite for the fathers be commenced with wisdom, O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  12. **The Abandonment of Worn Garments**

  Without which one does not come to earth, and without which nothing is carried away,
  having abandoned all self-earned wealth, both by merit and demerit. To indicate
  the departure, let the garment on the body be cast off, even before the rite of
  offering to the body, O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  13. **The Exchange of Self-Earned Wealth for Merit**

  When one performs desired and obligatory acts, one goes, having taken the fruit
  thereof. May one see, at the time of journeying to the other world, one''s own accumulated
  wealth. Being beautifully given to another, whether binding or unbinding, merit
  is purchased here by the rite of one''s own giving, O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  14. **Pūṣan and Savitā: Guides on the Path**

  May the learned Pūṣan, the protector of cattle, be ahead on the supreme path; and
  this Agni, the divine Savitā, be the protector, for knowledge from the gods. Leaders,
  knowers of the path to the world of the fathers, may they, full of love, lead you,
  O father. Come with happiness, never be afraid, O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  15. **Attainer of the World of Merit**

  Pūṣan, knower of the directions, will lead you to the world of merit, where these
  virtuous people shine, and attain the divine state. You will go to the world of
  the fathers, where the splendor of merit is distributed, free from sorrow. I salute
  those who are constantly engaged in the path to the other world, O Śrīrāma, Lord
  of Yoga.


  16. **The Return of the Wife to the World of the Living**

  "Hold your hand," may the wife, desiring your radiance, come, having endured immeasurable
  suffering, completely ceased from her duties, and exhausted. May she re-enter the
  world of the living, for the joy of her offspring. May we be victorious, may we
  cross over suffering quickly, O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  17. **The Consecration of the Sacrificial Vessels**

  The ladles filled with curds and ghee, and the other vessels, whatever is used in
  the rite of sacrifice, all of this is placed on the body. May Agni consecrate them,
  together with the entire body. You have attained the state of fire, may you conquer,
  O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  18. **The Offering Before Cremation**

  May the protectors, who are in various ways the vehicles to the world of the fathers,
  who are the guardians on all sides, the proclaimers, and those who speak with lamentation,
  accompanied by the sounds of praise, and Agni, who performs the action there, the
  wise one, and whatever we have not studied, may this offering of mine be to them,
  O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  19. **The Offering on the Chest**

  Just as the external fire is similar in form to the one dwelling within me, may
  it burn up everything contained within the body, all that is to be consumed, completely.
  At the end of the body, indeed, the entire frame crackles, and becomes ashes, a
  friend. To that, an offering is made on the chest, on the pyre, O Śrīrāma, Lord
  of Yoga.


  20. **The Movement of the Senses to Their Respective Deities**

  May the fiery essence of your eyes go to the sun, and the life-breath to the external
  air; may the gross and subtle elements return to the sky, and dissolve again into
  their cause. May the living being, you, enter the earth or the waters, or freely
  into the race of men. Having abandoned the former body, may you attain a new one,
  O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  21. **Agni, the Cremator, the Purifier of the Body**

  May the great fire burn here, with a tall banner of smoke, rising to the sky to
  lift up the birth-bound soul and lead it to heaven together. May that purifier,
  the electric fire, be victorious, leading you, blazing, to enjoy divine pleasures
  in the abode of the gods, O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  22. **The Third Divine Body**

  Having thus abandoned the old, worn-out body, you attain a second body of bones,
  and then a third, desired form, radiant with light, in heaven. You experience the
  pleasures attained by merit in many ways, constantly, with the gods. May you be
  worshipped, O noble, virtuous one, who has gone to heaven today, O Śrīrāma, Lord
  of Yoga.


  23. **Movement Through the Sky**

  Fly through the sky with boundless speed, like the bird Garutman. The two dogs,
  resembling the messengers of Yama, the two Sarameyas, are ahead of you. Go forward,
  beholding the vast, radiant Milky Way. You will shine, accomplish it quickly, O
  Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  24. **The Assembly in the Heavenly World**

  In heaven, you will see many Soma-sacrificers, those who offer honey and ghee, many
  kings, best of men, heroes who have given up their lives in battle, many ascetics,
  best of sages, and excellent celibate rulers. May the god establish you there in
  the abode of merit, O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.


  25. **Yama, the Giver of Rest**

  Those who have gone before, who have passed away and gone to their time, the messengers
  of the ender, and the new ones who are going, this lord is the son of Vivasvān,
  the ruler of the world. He is time itself, devoted to giving rest, the binder of
  the departed. This is the time to place the clod of earth and the bones, O Śrīrāma,
  Lord of Yoga.


  26. **Extinguishing the Fire on the Second Day**

  With milk and a stream of water, he who performed the rite of consecrating the fire
  for you, may I extinguish him, may there be peace for you on your journey, day by

  durva grass arise. May the suffering born of burning depart from that paternal forest,
  O Śrīrāma, Lord of Yoga.
