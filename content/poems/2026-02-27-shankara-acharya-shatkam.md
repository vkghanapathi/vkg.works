abstract: A devotional hymn of six stanzas in Sanskrit, Telugu, and Kannada composed
  by Dr. Vamshi Krishna Ghanapāṭhī in praise of Śaṅkara, the preeminent Advaita Vedānta
  philosopher and guru. The work celebrates Śaṅkara's role as the restorer of Vedic
  dharma, the defender of non-dual Brahman, and the supreme spiritual guide, while
  invoking his teachings on mokṣa and the unity of existence.
preamble: This composition belongs to the tradition of guru-stuti (guru-praise) and
  advaya-vāda (non-dual philosophy), drawing deeply from Advaita Vedānta as systematized
  by Ādi Śaṅkara in the 8th century. The hymn situates Śaṅkara within the lineage
  of Vedic interpreters (particularly referencing Bādarāyaṇa and the Brahma-sūtra-bhāṣya)
  and frames him as a liberator who dispels sectarian confusion and establishes the
  supremacy of the Absolute. Each stanza reiterates the refrain of seeking refuge
  in Śaṅkara as both cosmic and personal guide (bhuvī śaṅkaram, mam śaṅkaram), a classical
  devotional structure adapted to express philosophical devotion.

translation_en: '1.  Him I seek refuge in, Guru Śaṅkara, Śaṅkara on earth, my Śaṅkara,
  who is the cause for firmly establishing the Vedic Dharma, an eminent ascetic, who
  arose from Kerala, the uplifter of the entire Bharata continent, who awakened the
  essence of the Vedas, imperishable, transcending the near and far Himalayas.


  2.  Him I seek refuge in, Guru Śaṅkara, Śaṅkara on earth, my Śaṅkara, whose mind
  was auspiciously attached to Śrauta sacrifices, revered by magnanimous sacrificers,
  who taught the dedication to the Lord of the fruits of actions performed without
  attachment, who imparted the knowledge that the supreme truth is incomparable.


  3.  Him I seek refuge in, Guru Śaṅkara, Śaṅkara on earth, my Śaṅkara, who gave the
  understanding that people, with the notion that there are six faiths and many deities,
  undergo suffering and engage in constant strife and ritualistic conflicts, should
  not be concerned, for there is only one divinity.


  4.  Him I seek refuge in, Guru Śaṅkara, Śaṅkara on earth, my Śaṅkara, whose foremost
  step is the desire to serve the Lord, whose praise is extensively sung with countless
  names, who composed many hymns, whose delightful verses are filled with the nectar
  of truth, an ocean of compassion.


  5.  Him I seek refuge in, Guru Śaṅkara, Śaṅkara on earth, my Śaṅkara, whose great
  work is the commentary on the aphorisms of the silent Bādarāyaṇa, who firmly established
  the knowledge that Brahman is non-dual and that anything other than it is illusory,
  who was exceedingly skilled in defeating all opposing debaters with the strength
  of his own reasoning.


  6.  By praising Guru Śaṅkara, the supreme refuge, one will attain it; a devotee
  should understand the teachings of Guru Śaṅkara. By whose grace, that supreme state,
  filled with excellent devotion, is easily attainable; Him I seek refuge in, Guru
  Śaṅkara, Śaṅkara on earth, my Śaṅkara.'
