abstract: A devotional hymn of six stanzas in Sanskrit, Telugu, and Kannada composed
  by Dr. Vamshi Krishna Ghanapāṭhī in praise of Śaṅkara, the preeminent Advaita Vedānta
  philosopher and guru. The work celebrates Śaṅkara's role as the restorer of Vedic
  dharma, the defender of non-dual Brahman, and the supreme spiritual guide, while
  invoking his teachings on mokṣa and the unity of existence.
preamble: This composition belongs to the tradition of guru-stuti (guru-praise) and
  advaya-vāda (non-dual philosophy), drawing deeply from Advaita Vedānta as systematized
  by Ādi Śaṅkara in the 8th century. The hymn situates Śaṅkara within the lineage
  of Vedic interpreters (particularly referencing Bādarāyaṇa and the Brahma-sūtra-bhāṣya)
  and frames him as a liberator who dispels sectarian confusion and establishes the
  supremacy of the Absolute. Each stanza reiterates the refrain of seeking refuge
  in Śaṅkara as both cosmic and personal guide (bhuvī śaṅkaram, mam śaṅkaram), a classical
  devotional structure adapted to express philosophical devotion.

translation_en: '1.  Him I seek refuge in, Guru Śaṅkara, Śaṅkara on earth, my Śaṅkara,
  who is the cause for firmly establishing the Vedic Dharma, an eminent ascetic, who
  arose from Kerala, the uplifter of the entire Bharata continent, who awakens the
  essence of the Vedas, beyond the reach of the Himalayas.


  2.  Him I seek refuge in, Guru Śaṅkara, Śaṅkara on earth, my Śaṅkara, who bestows
  auspiciousness upon those whose minds are well-disposed towards Śrauta sacrifices,
  upon the great-souled sacrificers, who teaches that the fruits of performed actions
  should be surrendered to the Lord, and that the supreme truth is incomparable.


  3.  Him I seek refuge in, Guru Śaṅkara, Śaṅkara on earth, my Śaṅkara, who gives
  the understanding that people, with the notion that there are six faiths and many
  deities, undergo suffering constantly, engaging in quarrelsome rituals, and who
  instructs, "Do not respect this; there is only one deity here."


  4.  Him I seek refuge in, Guru Śaṅkara, Śaṅkara on earth, my Śaṅkara, whose foremost
  step is the desire to serve the Lord, whose glorification is celebrated with countless
  names in extensive, multifaceted praises, whose words are delightful, a nectar of
  truth, an ocean of compassion.


  5.  Him I seek refuge in, Guru Śaṅkara, Śaṅkara on earth, my Śaṅkara, whose great
  work is the commentary on the aphorisms of the silent Bādarāyaṇa, who gives the
  understanding that Brahman is non-dual and that anything other than it is false,
  who is exceedingly skilled in defeating all opposing debaters with the strength
  of his own reasoning.


  6.  From this hymn, one will attain the supreme refuge of the Guru; a devotee deserves
  understanding in the words of Śaṅkara, the Guru. By whose grace, that supreme state
  is easily attainable; Him I seek refuge in, Guru Śaṅkara, Śaṅkara on earth, my Śaṅkara.'
